## 1. Ephesians 2:8–9

### Verses

- **1611 KJV:** "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and **that** not of yourselves: **it** is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast."

- **TR (Beza 1598):** τῇ γὰρ **χάριτί** ἐστε σεσῳσμένοι διὰ τῆς **πίστεως**· καὶ **τοῦτο** οὐκ ἐξ ὑμῶν· θεοῦ τὸ δῶρον·

### Greek Grammatical Proof (Gender Mismatch)

1. **χάριτί ("grace")** → Dative, **Feminine**, Singular (3rd word).

2. **πίστεως ("faith")** → Genitive, **Feminine**, Singular (7th word).

3. **τοῦτο ("that")** → Nominative/Accusative, **Neuter**, Singular (9th word).

In Greek syntax, a demonstrative pronoun must agree in gender with its noun antecedent. Because **πίστεως** ("faith") is feminine and **τοῦτο** ("that") is neuter, **τοῦτο** cannot grammatically refer to "faith." Instead, the neuter pronoun refers back to the entire preceding clause as an abstract concept: the entire process of being saved by grace through faith.

### English Grammatical Proof (Clause Anaphora & Parallel Modifiers)

1. **Clause-Level Anaphora:** In English syntax, when "that" introduces a parenthetical coordinate clause (*"and that not of yourselves"*) followed by *"it is the gift of God,"* the pronouns "that" and "it" function as clause-level pronouns. If "that" modified "faith" specifically, standard syntax would require a relative pronoun (*"through faith, which is not of yourselves"*).

2. **Parallel Prepositional Modifiers:** Verse 9 attaches a second parallel prepositional phrase to the same antecedent: *"Not of works."* In Biblical English, faith is contrasted *against* works (e.g., Romans 3:28), whereas **salvation** is "not of works." Because both "not of yourselves" (v. 8) and "not of works" (v. 9) modify the same referent, the referent must be the full predicate *"by grace are ye saved through faith."*

### Conclusive Resolution

Both Greek gender agreement rules and English clause structures strictly forbid "faith" from being the isolated antecedent of "that." Both tongues lock "that/it" to the overarching gift of salvation by grace through faith.

## 2. Acts 2:38

### Verses

- **1611 KJV:** "Then Peter said unto them, **Repent**, and **be baptized every one of you** in the name of Jesus Christ **for the remission of sins**, and **ye shall receive** the gift of the Holy Ghost."

- **TR (Beza 1598):** Πέτρος δὲ ἔφη πρὸς αὐτούς, **Μετανοήσατε**, καὶ **βαπτισθήτω ἕκαστος ὑμῶν** ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν· καὶ **λήψεσθε** τὴν δωρεὰν τοῦ Ἁγίου Πνεύματος.

### Greek Grammatical Proof (Verbal Person/Number Framing)

1. **Μετανοήσατε ("Repent")** → Aorist Active Imperative, **2nd Person Plural**.

2. **βαπτισθήτω ἕκαστος ("be baptized every one")** → Aorist Passive Imperative, **3rd Person Singular** (**βαπτισθήτω**) + Nominative **Masculine Singular** Indefinite Pronoun (**ἕκαστος**).

3. **λήψεσθε ("ye shall receive")** → Future Middle Deponent Indicative, **2nd Person Plural**.

The main sentence structure is driven by the 2nd-person plural frame: **Μετανοήσατε** (plural command) ... **λήψεσθε** (plural promise). The phrase **εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν** ("for the remission of sins") anchors back to the primary 2nd-person plural command ("Repent ye"), while the 3rd-person singular command (**βαπτισθήτω ἕκαστος**) stands as a parenthetical individual instruction inserted within that plural address.

### English Grammatical Proof (Narrative Frame & Prepositional Attachment)

1. **Plural Narrative Frame:** English imperatives track person and number explicitly:

   - **"Repent"** = Plural command addressed to the whole crowd (*"Repent [ye]"*).

   - **"every one of you"** = Distributive Singular subject governing *"be baptized"*.

   - **"ye shall receive"** = 2nd-Person Plural pronoun (*"ye [all]"*).

2. **Prepositional Attachment:** In English compound sentence logic, when a main plural command ("Repent") is separated from a plural promise ("ye shall receive") by an inserted singular directive ("be baptized every one of you"), the primary purpose clause (**"for the remission of sins"**) attaches to the primary plural command that governs the entire assembly ("Repent").

### Conclusive Resolution

Both Greek verbal parsing and English pronoun framing isolate "be baptized every one of you" as a 3rd-person singular insertion. Remission of sins is grammatically locked to the 2nd-person plural command "Repent."

## 3. Matthew 16:18

### Verses

- **1611 KJV:** "And I say also unto **thee**, That **thou** art **Peter**, and upon **this rock** I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it."

- **TR (Beza 1598):** κἀγὼ δέ σοι λέγω ὅτι σὺ εἶ **Πέτρος**, καὶ ἐπὶ **ταύτῃ τῇ πέτρᾳ** οἰκοδομήσω μου τὴν ἐκκλησίαν, καὶ πύλαι ᾅδου οὐ κατισχύσουσιν αὐτῆς.

### Greek Grammatical Proof (Gender Barrier)

1. **Πέτρος ("Peter")** → Proper Noun, Nominative, **Masculine**, Singular.

2. **ταύτῃ τῇ πέτρᾳ ("this rock")** → Demonstrative Pronoun + Noun, Dative, **Feminine**, Singular.

In Greek, if the demonstrative pronoun **ταύτῃ** ("this") were intended to point directly to the masculine proper noun **Πέτρος**, syntax strictly required the masculine demonstrative **τούτῳ** (*"upon this [man]"*). The deliberate shift to the feminine demonstrative phrase **ταύτῃ τῇ πέτρᾳ** creates a structural barrier, referring back instead to the feminine concept of Peter's confession in verse 16 or to Christ Himself.

### English Grammatical Proof (Person Shift & Proximal Demonstrative)

1. **Shift in Personal Address:** Jesus addresses Peter directly using 2nd-person pronouns: *"unto **thee**"* and *"**thou** art Peter."* Had Jesus intended to identify Peter as the foundational rock, standard English syntax for direct address required continuing the 2nd-person pronoun: *"and upon **thee** I will build my church."* Shifting to the 3rd-person phrase *"this rock"* breaks direct address and points to a different subject.

2. **Proximal Demonstrative Alignment:** "This" is a proximal demonstrative adjective pointing to the immediate topic of discourse. In context, two entities exist: Peter (addressed directly as "thou") and the revealed truth of Christ's identity (v. 16–17: *"Thou art the Christ... flesh and blood hath not revealed **it** unto thee"*). Because "thou" is locked to Peter, "this rock" points back to the revealed truth of Christ's deity.

### Conclusive Resolution

The Greek gender shift (**Πέτρος** vs. **πέτρᾳ**) and the English person shift ("thou" vs. "this rock") both independently forbid identifying Peter as the rock upon which the Church is built.
